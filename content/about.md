Outside of work, my favourite "pastime" is travelling. I love exploring new places, and I often try to find the less touristy ones. I also play chess, I am fascinated by hypnosis, and I am learning the keyboard and the guitar, although I have been quite out of touch with the latter two over the past few years.

## Travel and photography

So far, I have visited 17 states in the US and, back home in India, 16 of the 28 states and 3 of the 8 union territories. Conferences and research visits have also taken me to Japan and across eastern Canada. Kyoto remains my favourite place of all. In Japan, I sprinted through Osaka, Kyoto, and Tokyo in just three days. It was an experience in itself, and it was probably only possible because of the incredible reliability of the Shinkansen.

I photograph most of these trips on an iPhone. I find that a phone camera, with careful attention to angles and a little colour grading afterwards, can produce photographs of remarkable quality. I am particularly drawn to streets, transit systems, waterfronts, and landscapes.

[See a selection of my photographs on Instagram (@deb.roaming) →](https://www.instagram.com/deb.roaming/)

## Chess

I play chess regularly, mostly 10-minute rapid games online on Lichess. I particularly like the King's Gambit, and I also play the London System (when I want to stabilize my rating!).

I also absolutely love the banter around the Bongcloud, and I have seen plenty of creative ideas for tricking opponents out of it. As you might expect, winning with the Bongcloud can have a deep psychological impact on your opponent. Magnus once opened against Wesley with a variant of the Bongcloud in a 2020 blitz game, and Wesley later called his [defeat a humbling experience](https://www.chess.com/blog/ThummimS/barnes-opening).

I am always happy to play a game with friends and colleagues, so feel free to send me a challenge.

## Hypnosis

I am very interested in hypnosis, and I have learnt a few inductions, both rapid inductions and the slower progressive relaxation ones. Whenever I talk about hypnosis, I am fascinated by how many people are either intimidated or wary of it. Despite its reputation, hypnosis and the state of trance can actually lead to a great level of relaxation.

You can also try self-hypnosis. There are lots of videos on YouTube, though they probably won't work unless you are in a very calm environment. Here are two that I think are the best I have seen, and they are pretty underrated compared to the popular ones. If you have a suggestion that you think is better, or one that worked really well for you, please let me know!

- [Hypnotize Yourself, Extra Deep Hypnosis](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ahOzV0pKWQk) by Amazing Hypnosis
- [Deep Trance Rejuvenation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VudAUF1-oQs) by Trigram Healing

If you are planning to try hypnosis with someone, make sure it is someone you trust, and someone who will always take the time to properly guide you out of trance at the end. No, it is not that scary! You will *always* come out of it, and a good hypnotist will often leave you with a positive post-hypnotic suggestion (a helpful thought planted during trance that stays with you afterwards), so you might actually feel a lot better than your baseline. If you want a short introduction, I really recommend the book [*Reality is Plastic*](https://books.google.com/books/about/Reality_is_Plastic.html?id=Vz6Z0AEACAAJ) by Anthony Jacquin.

A couple of common misconceptions about hypnosis

- No one can get you to do anything that you do not intend to do during hypnosis.
- You are awake the whole time. It is nothing like the movies!
- Hypnosis is not sleep. You can hear, think, and usually remember everything that happens.
- You cannot get "stuck" in hypnosis. Even if the hypnotist simply walked away, you would drift out of trance on your own.

I love conversations about hypnosis, whether it is how it has impacted you or just how you feel about it in general, so please feel free to chat with me about anything related to hypnosis!
